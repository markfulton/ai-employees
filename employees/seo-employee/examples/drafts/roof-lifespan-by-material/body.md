# How Long Does a Roof Last? Lifespan by Material

A roof lasts anywhere from fifteen years to well past fifty, and the material decides most of that range. Three tab asphalt shingles sit at the short end, architectural asphalt in the middle, and metal, cedar shake, and clay or concrete tile at the long end. Ventilation, the pitch of the roof, how much shade and moss it carries, and whether small repairs were done on time move every one of those figures up or down.

## How long does an asphalt shingle roof last?

Three tab shingles usually give fifteen to twenty years. Architectural shingles, which are thicker and layered, usually give twenty five to thirty. The published [service life figures for common roofing materials](https://www.example.org/roofing-material-service-life) put asphalt at the bottom of the table for one reason: the granules that protect the mat wear away, and once they go the shingle dries out and cracks. A roof that bakes in full sun all afternoon loses them faster than one on a shaded north slope.

## How long do metal, cedar, and tile roofs last?

Standing seam metal runs forty to seventy years, and the panels rarely fail before the fasteners and the sealant around penetrations do. Cedar shake gives twenty five to forty years when it can dry out between rains, and far less when moss holds water against it. Clay and concrete tile can pass fifty years, but the underlayment beneath the tile does not, so a tile roof usually needs one relay of the underlayment in its life while the tiles themselves are reused.

## What shortens the life of a roof?

Poor attic ventilation is the most common cause we find. Heat trapped under the deck cooks the shingles from below and the roof ages from both sides at once. After that come clogged gutters, moss left to grow, trees that scrape the surface in wind, and flashing that was never sealed properly around a chimney or a skylight. None of those are expensive to fix early, and all of them are expensive to ignore.

## How do you know a roof is near the end?

Look for shingles that curl at the edges, bald patches where the granules are gone, and daylight through the boards in the attic. Inspectors also read the age of a roof from the condition of the flashing and the ridge, and the guide to [how inspectors judge the age of a roof](https://www.example.net/home-inspection/roof-age-signs) explains what they look for. ## Frequently asked questions

**Does the warranty tell me how long the roof will last?** No. A warranty covers manufacturing defects in the shingle, not wear from weather, and most run out long before the roof does.

**Can a roof outlast its typical range?** Yes. A well ventilated, well maintained roof on a simple gable often does, while a badly ventilated one on a complex roofline can fail years early.

**Is it worth repairing a roof that is near the end?** If the damage is local, such as after a storm, a repair buys time. Our guide to [storm damage roof repair](/blog/storm-damage-roof-repair) covers when a repair makes sense and when it does not.
